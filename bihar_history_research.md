# BIHAR HISTORY: LAST 500 YEARS (1526-2026)

## INTRODUCTION

Bihar, located in eastern India, has a rich and complex history spanning over five centuries. This research document covers the period from 1526 to 2026, encompassing the medieval period under various dynasties, British colonial rule, and the post-independence era. Bihar has been a cradle of ancient civilizations, a center of religious movements, and a significant participant in India's freedom struggle.

## MEDIEVAL PERIOD (1526-1757)

### Early Mughal Era (1526-1540)

With the establishment of the Mughal Empire by Babur in 1526, Bihar came under Mughal influence. However, the region's strategic location and wealth made it a contested territory among various powers.

### Sur Dynasty and Sher Shah Suri (1540-1555)

The most significant development in Bihar during this period was the rise of Sher Shah Suri (1486-1545), originally named Farid Khan. Key points:

- **Background**: Son of Hasan Khan, a jagirdar of Sasaram in Bihar
- **Title**: Given the title "Sher Khan" for his bravery under Afghan rule
- **Achievements**: 
  - Defeated combined forces of Lohani chiefs of Bihar and Mahmud Shah of Bengal at Surajgarh
  - Whole of Bihar came under Sher Shah's control
  - Established the Sur dynasty by dethroning Humayun in 1540
  - Ruled as Sultan of Hindustan from 1540 to 1545
- **Administrative Reforms**: 
  - Reorganized the empire laying foundations for later Mughal emperors
  - Introduced the currency of rupee
  - Established effective administrative systems
- **Capital**: Sasaram in modern-day Bihar

### Later Mughal Period (1555-1757)

After the restoration of Mughal rule under Akbar, Bihar became an important province (suba) of the Mughal Empire. The region saw:

- **Administrative Integration**: Bihar was formally integrated into the Mughal imperial structure
- **Economic Development**: The region continued to be agriculturally prosperous
- **Cultural Synthesis**: Mughal influence blended with local traditions
- **Decline of Central Authority**: By the early 18th century, Mughal control weakened, leading to regional power struggles

## BRITISH COLONIAL PERIOD (1757-1947)

### Early British Rule (1757-1857)

#### Battle of Plassey and Aftermath

- **1757**: Battle of Plassey established East India Company dominance in Bengal, which included Bihar
- **1765**: Bihar came under British rule through the Diwani rights granted to the East India Company
- **Administrative Changes**: Bihar merged with Bengal Presidency, losing its independent identity

#### Resistance and Revolts

- **Chota Nagpur Uprisings**: Tribal revolts against British authority
  - Ho revolt (1820-1827)
  - Munda uprising (1831-1832)
- **1857 Rebellion**: Bihar played an important role in the Indian Mutiny

### Freedom Movement in Bihar (1857-1947)

#### Early Nationalism (1885-1919)

Bihar actively participated in the successive phases of Indian nationalism. The region produced several prominent freedom fighters and political leaders.

#### Gandhian Era and Mass Movements (1919-1947)

**Champaran Satyagraha (1917)**:
- **Historical Significance**: First civil resistance movement led by Mahatma Gandhi in India
- **Background**: Farmers in Champaran district protested against forced indigo cultivation
- **Systems**: Panchkathia or Teenkathia system forced tenant farmers to grow indigo on portions of their land
- **Gandhi's Role**: Upon returning from South Africa in 1915, Gandhi organized mass uprisings against injustice
- **Impact**: Gave direction to India's youth and freedom struggle, establishing satyagraha as a powerful tool

**Other Movements**:
- **Non-Cooperation Movement (1920-1922)**: Massive participation from Bihar
- **Civil Disobedience Movement (1930-1934)**: Salt March and related activities
- **Quit India Movement (1942)**: Strong support and participation

#### Key Leaders from Bihar

- **Rajendra Prasad**: Born in Siwan district, became the first President of independent India
- **Jayaprakash Narayan**: Prominent socialist leader
- **Anugrah Narayan Sinha**: Key figure in Bihar's politics
- **Sri Krishna Sinha**: First Chief Minister of Bihar

### Administrative Changes

- **1912**: Province of Bihar and Orissa formed, separating from Bengal Presidency
- **1936**: Bihar and Orissa became separate provinces
- **1948**: Small states of Saraikela and Kharsawan merged with Bihar

## POST-INDEPENDENCE ERA (1947-2026)

### Early Years (1947-1970)

#### Political Development

- **1947**: Bihar became a constituent part of independent India
- **1950**: Established as a state in the Indian Union
- **1956**: Linguistic reorganization - territory transferred to West Bengal
- **Political Leadership**: Dominated by Indian National Congress under leaders like Sri Krishna Sinha

#### Economic and Social Changes

- **Land Reforms**: Implementation of land ceiling and redistribution policies
- **Industrial Development**: Initial efforts to industrialize the state
- **Educational Expansion**: Establishment of new educational institutions

### Period of Challenges (1970-1990)

#### Political Instability

- **1970s-1980s**: Increasing political instability and fragmentation
- **Economic Stagnation**: Structural weaknesses in the economy became apparent
- **Social Tensions**: Rising caste-based politics and social divisions

#### Economic Issues

- **Agricultural Dependence**: Heavy reliance on agriculture with limited diversification
- **Industrial Backwardness**: Slow industrial growth compared to other states
- **Infrastructure Deficits**: Poor infrastructure development

### Era of Change (1990-2000)

#### Political Transformation

- **1990**: First time since independence, a state government elected from a party other than the ruling national party
- **Rise of Regional Parties**: Emergence of parties like RJD and JD(U)
- **Social Justice**: Implementation of Mandal Commission recommendations

#### Major Development

- **2000**: Creation of Jharkhand state from southern Bihar (Chota Nagpur plateau region)
- **Impact**: Bihar lost mineral-rich areas and industrial centers

### Recent Period (2000-2026)

#### Economic Turnaround Attempts

- **2005-2015**: Period of economic turnaround attempts under various governments
- **Development Initiatives**: Focus on infrastructure, education, and healthcare
- **Growth Patterns**: Attempts to break from structural constraints

#### Political Landscape

- **Coalition Politics**: Dominated by coalition governments
- **Key Parties**: RJD, JD(U), BJP, Congress
- **Leadership Changes**: Multiple Chief Ministers and political realignments

#### Contemporary Challenges

- **Development Gaps**: Persistent poverty and underdevelopment
- **Infrastructure Needs**: Continued requirements for better infrastructure
- **Human Development**: Challenges in education, healthcare, and employment

## CULTURAL AND SOCIAL DEVELOPMENTS

### Religious Significance

- **Buddhism**: Bodh Gaya remains one of the most important Buddhist pilgrimage sites
- **Jainism**: Historical connections to Mahavira and Jain traditions
- **Hinduism**: Numerous important temples and religious sites

### Educational Evolution

- **Ancient Learning Centers**: Nalanda and Vikramashila universities (though earlier, their legacy continued)
- **Modern Education**: Development of universities and colleges in the colonial and post-colonial periods

### Social Changes

- **Caste Dynamics**: Evolution of caste-based politics and social movements
- **Women's Empowerment**: Gradual improvements in women's participation in public life
- **Urbanization**: Slow but steady urban growth and development

## ECONOMIC HISTORY

### Agricultural Economy

- **Traditional Base**: Historically dependent on agriculture
- **Green Revolution**: Impact and limitations in Bihar
- **Land Reforms**: Various attempts at agricultural reform

### Industrial Development

- **Colonial Period**: Limited industrialization under British rule
- **Post-Independence**: Various industrial policy initiatives
- **Recent Efforts**: Attempts to attract investment and develop industries

### Service Sector Growth

- **IT and Services**: Emerging opportunities in the service sector
- **Government Employment**: Significant role of government jobs in the economy

## CONCLUSION

Bihar's history over the last 500 years reflects the broader patterns of Indian history while maintaining its unique character. From the glory of Sher Shah Suri's empire to the struggles of colonial rule and the challenges of post-independence development, Bihar has played a significant role in shaping India's destiny.

The state's journey from a medieval powerhouse to a colonial province and then to a modern Indian state demonstrates remarkable resilience and adaptability. Despite facing numerous challenges, Bihar continues to evolve and contribute to India's cultural, political, and economic landscape.

As we look toward the future, Bihar's rich historical legacy provides both inspiration and lessons for addressing contemporary challenges and realizing its full potential in the decades to come.
